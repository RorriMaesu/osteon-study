## Earlier curriculum review

The following findings and priorities record the review of release 8e3c546 across the 49 numbered sections of OpenStax Chapters 12–20. They describe the course at that release. [Open the current course](https://rorrimaesu.github.io/osteon-study/#course-2)

## 1. Fix what could teach the wrong lesson

| Finding in the current course | Revision | How to check it |
|---|---|---|
| The §12.4 exam spike has a numbered voltage axis with inconsistent spacing. The drawn distance for 30 mV exceeds the distance for 70 mV. | Use one linear voltage scale for the axis, peak, trough and X marker. Keep the trace available without a running animation. | Check the positions of +30, 0 and −70 mV and place X on the descending trace. Ask which current drives that phase. |
| The §14.1 exam expects *accommodation*. The lesson says the lens adjusts focus. §16.2 tests *anterograde amnesia* without explicitly teaching the distinction from retrograde amnesia. | Teach both concepts before testing them. Show near/far focusing and a before/after memory timeline. Link the eye lesson to §16.3. | Ask the learner to reverse the focus change or interpret a fresh memory history. A student should be able to explain both from the assigned material. |
| The §20.5 route display gives every venous station the same valve-shaped icon, including pulmonary and portal veins. The systemic lab traces only the left upper limb. | Use a neutral icon for veins. Show valves at suitable limb locations. Add arterial supply to the foot and both deep and superficial examples of the return trip. | Follow a vessel through a change of anatomical name, from a different viewing angle, with the upcoming stops hidden. |
| The §19.4 grader accepts 5.46 and rejects 5.46 L/min. The field asks for a number, but both expressions communicate the expected output. | Accept an explicitly matching unit. Where the objective is calculating output, accept equivalent converted amounts such as 5460 mL/min. For a question explicitly testing conversion to a requested unit, give targeted unit feedback. | Test correct numbers with and without units, correct conversions, incompatible dimensions and factor-of-1000 errors. Preserve the original record on previously submitted attempts. |

Primary references for these revisions are [the action potential](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-4-the-action-potential), [the cranial nerve exam](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/16-3-the-cranial-nerve-exam), [mental status](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/16-2-the-mental-status-exam), [cardiac physiology](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-4-cardiac-physiology), and [circulatory pathways](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-5-circulatory-pathways).

## 2. Teach each chapter through a task students can perform

| Chapter | What students can already do | What to teach and how to assess it next |
|---|---|---|
| **12 · Nervous tissue** | Inspect a multipolar neuron, change the depolarizing input, and compare summation at the initial segment. | Contrast pseudounipolar, bipolar and multipolar neurons and compare myelinating glia. In a separate magnified axon, trace local current under the myelin and spike regeneration at a node. Switch to a synapse and compare chemical release with direct current through a gap junction. Have students distinguish receptor, generator and postsynaptic potentials in a fresh example and predict what changing transmitter or receptor changes. [§12.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-5-communication-between-neurons) |
| **13 · Nervous system anatomy** | Follow CSF through a side-view schematic, inspect a blocked aqueduct, and compare dorsal and ventral roots. | Connect the CSF lesson to Osteon’s existing paired lateral ventricles, third ventricle, cerebral aqueduct and fourth ventricle. Add sagittal, coronal and axial orientation, then progressively uncover the brain around the same target. After an aqueduct block, ask which spaces can enlarge upstream and which ventricle is downstream. Give cerebral arteries their own route and compare interrupted perfusion with interrupted CSF flow. Connect brain-vesicle development and basal nuclei to identifiable adult sections. [§13.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-2-the-central-nervous-system) · [§13.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-3-circulation-and-the-central-nervous-system) |

For near focus, contract the ciliary muscle, reduce zonular tension and round the lens. Then ask for the reverse sequence. In an ear cutaway animate a pressure wave, angular acceleration and linear acceleration separately. In central processing, extend the existing spinal routes to a visual-field task where the student distinguishes a visual hemifield from one eye. Test from a new orientation with the body’s right and left clearly marked. [§14.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception) · [§14.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-2-central-processing) |

Ask where a hypothetical receptor blockade interrupts signaling and which response remains. Use distinct symbols for a nerve ending and hormone entering blood. Some distal vagal connections require separate anatomical verification. [§15.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/15-1-divisions-of-the-autonomic-nervous-system) · [§15.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/15-2-autonomic-reflexes-and-homeostasis) |

At another, compare eye abduction, a visual-field pattern and a light reflex. Reuse the Motion Lab for normal reach, overshoot and gait. Ask for the observed finding, one plausible localization, and the next observation that distinguishes two explanations. Score description and reasoning separately. Include a keyboard-controlled sequence and a timing-equivalent text description. [§16.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/16-2-the-mental-status-exam) · [§16.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/16-5-the-coordination-and-gait-exams) |

Ask students to identify a gland, predict an upstream and downstream change, then explain the result. For the pancreas, open a separately scaled islet to distinguish alpha and beta cells and nearby exocrine tissue. [§17.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/17-1-an-overview-of-the-endocrine-system) · [§17.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/17-2-hormones) |

## 3. Choose the 3D sources and the right scale

All 49 sections have a diagram and guided practice. Twenty-five have an additional interactive activity, and several of those activities serve more than one section. Six of the 49 lesson reading pages have a direct named-structure Atlas link. All six are in Chapters 12–16. Students can already open the shared Atlas more broadly. The existing nervous-system geometry is the quickest route to better spatial practice. The first substantial new organ I would build is the heart. ## 4. Make the exams measure transfer

- **Change what gets sampled.** The 186-item bank contains 159 section-recall items plus 27 applied items. Every 67-question cumulative form draws 49 questions directly from lesson recall and the same nine visual items, one per chapter. Mixing the answer order helps, but students can remember the illustrations. Keep the familiar questions for practice. Reserve different diagrams, parameters, viewing angles or cases for the independent exam. For a tract crossing, ask about the opposite side and a different lesion level. For the heart, give new pressures and require a valve prediction. Variable examples during retrieval have improved transfer in controlled studies. [Exam selection](https://github.com/RorriMaesu/osteon-study/blob/8e3c546/src/ap2-exam-core.js) · [Butler and colleagues, experimental study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29265856/)
- **Blueprint by task.** The current cumulative selection produces 13 endocrine questions and 5 somatic nervous system questions, largely because their chapters have 11 and 3 sections. Record separate targets for identification, route tracing, graph or micrograph interpretation, and explanation. Adjust priorities when the instructor supplies a syllabus or lab list.
- **Require a prediction before revealing the mechanism.** The common guided sequence shows a prompt and an explanation on demand. Let students commit to a choice or short prediction in the cases that matter. Show the consequence, explain the tempting alternative, and offer a different example a few questions later. Keep unassisted performance, assisted practice and self-marked reading distinct.
- **Use the nine written cases twice.** Keep the worked example and rubric for study. Give the exam a different case testing the same reasoning. Present evidence for each rubric point independently from the automatic score. Let the Socratic tutor ask for the missing observation after submission and carry its source section into feedback.
- **Offer equivalent practical access.** Provide enlarge, reset, orientation markers, optional labels, one-step rewind, play/pause, speed selection and reduced-motion frames. Pair color with arrows, line styles and text. Let students choose a named target by keyboard or touch. Hide answer-bearing labels consistently while retaining a neutral description of the visible evidence. Test whether a student can answer on a small phone at normal zoom as well as on a laptop.

## 5. Release in an order students can evaluate

1. **Correct the four teaching and assessment problems above.** Add paired unseen questions for focus, memory, graph reading and lower-limb circulation. Recheck known submitted records and their original scoring.
2. **Ship the ventricular practical using current models.** Provide one whole-brain view, one cutaway and one unseen section. A learner should trace CSF in order and predict the effect of an aqueduct obstruction without a hint.
3. **Prototype the heart with a single synchronized heartbeat.** Start with one side and validate every pressure crossing. Check S1, S2, ventricular volume and the two intervals when all valves are closed. Add the right side, a conduction overlay and coronary anatomy after the cutaway passes that review.
4. **Add the eye, ear and regional vessels in separate releases.** Each release should include an orientation task, a manipulated condition, and a new independent practical. Add the gland locator and blood microscopy station next. Reserve embryonic morphing for a separate phase with staged anatomical references.
5. **Verify each release inside the existing body.** Before every Blender change to an existing model, make a dated backup, record its checksum and confirm it reopens. Edit a working copy and export a separately loadable layer. Check alignment in three planes, neighboring structures, laterality, flow direction and extreme animation frames against named references. Load only the selected organ on phones. Test on an actual phone and a laptop, with labels hidden, zoom buttons available, and a clear return to the lesson. Have an anatomy instructor or lab demonstrator review representative frames and a student attempt the new practical cold before labeling a model ready for scored identification.

A useful pilot comparison is the same learner identifying a structure from a familiar view, then explaining its function in a novel view a few days later. Record which structures they confuse, the hints they use and which prompts they misread. Those observations give the next revision a better target than a count of figures or animations alone.
