## Whole-body specimen

`registered-organs.glb` contains 157 named surfaces arranged in 50 selectable organ and tissue groups. It uses the same frame as the Osteon skeleton. The patient's left is positive X, superior is positive Y, and anterior is positive Z. The units are meters. The skin is an optional surface assembled from the source atlas's regional skin patches. [Organ groups and source fingerprints](../../src/organ-atlas-assets.json)

The outer skin uses 250 native Z-Anatomy patches, including the eyebrows, philtrum and lip margins. The Blender export takes the original exterior polygons before the regional thickness modifiers. It joins coincident boundary vertices at micrometre precision, omits 40 duplicate polygons and preserves the donor positions and eyelid openings. An update to this skin alone verifies that the other 491 geometry buffer views stay byte-identical. The viewer also corrects the facing direction of verified mirrored organ surfaces, including the cerebral folds. [Blender skin exporter](../../scripts/blender/refresh_registered_skin.py)

Most surfaces come from **Z-Anatomy – The libre 3D atlas of anatomy**, Gauthier Kervyn and contributors, based on **BodyParts3D**, Kousaku Okubo and the Database Center for Life Science. Osteon selected and named the surfaces, merged some regional pieces, simplified their triangle counts and exported their positions in Blender 4.5.1. Z-Anatomy's adaptations are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. The upstream collection also credits BodyParts3D under its original CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan terms, Brainder and University of Washington white matter anatomy. The inner-ear components, including the paired cochleae in this file, retain the University of Dundee School of Medicine's **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** terms for *Anatomy of the Inner Ear*. Individual components retain their respective licenses when the collection is shared. The kidneys, jejunum, ileum, cecum and rectum are separate exports from the indexed BodyParts3D version 4.0 OBJ release. **BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International**. Osteon aligned both kidneys to their respective atlas kidney envelopes. A single translation places all four bowel segments relative to six corresponding abdominal and bony landmarks. Their landmark center differences range from 1.3 to 12.8 mm. These gross organ surfaces illustrate regional relations. Separate surfaces can have small gaps at junctions. ## Female urinary specimen

`female-urinary.glb` includes 37 structures from the Human Reference Atlas versions 1.2 of the female bladder and both ureters. Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein created the reference organs from the National Library of Medicine's Visible Human data. Douglas Strand reviewed the reference anatomy. Osteon combined the meshes at their supplied transforms and simplified their surfaces. The ureters approach their corresponding ureteric openings within 2 mm at the nearest exported vertices. The bladder, ureters and Osteon's existing HRA female ovarian and pelvic structures share the Visible Human Female frame. This preparation opens in its female specimen frame. [Bladder](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM595.LMKX.573) · [Left ureter](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM343.JGCD.345) · [Right ureter](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM375.FNHZ.563) · [Pelvic specimen credits](../ap2-glands/SOURCES.md) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Mammary gland specimen

`mammary-glands.glb` combines 16 named structures from Heidi Schlehlein's bilateral version 1.1 Human Reference Atlas breast models, reviewed by Harikrishna Nakshatri. These are atlas-based anatomical reconstructions. The segmented fat, lobes, major milk ducts, nipples, areolae, areolar tubercles and suspensory ligaments retain the source positions. Osteon reduced the surface density while preserving more detail around lobes and ducts. This is a regional specimen. Any particles shown in a lesson illustrate a process and direction, with the anatomy available separately for inspection. [Left breast](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM533.BNJL.834) · [Right breast](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM235.CHRK.259) · [Human Reference Atlas](https://humanatlas.io/3d-reference-library) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
## Organ surface appearance

**Natural tissue** gives selected gross surfaces an illustrative finish. For the liver, the smooth red-brown external surface and restrained sheen follow normal human specimen photographs. The separately modeled portal vein uses a muted vessel-wall finish. No capillary network or individual liver lobules have been drawn on the capsule. [Normal human liver specimen, Fig. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6448666/) · [Monash University liver anatomy](https://epath.med.monash.edu.au/page1438.html)

The lungs and diaphragm retain the source fissures and muscle contour. The heart walls, gastrointestinal tract, kidneys, glands and nervous tissue use broad, low-contrast variation. Thoracic, abdominal and head color cryosections provide visual context. Exact hue, gloss and variation are illustrative and vary with perfusion, preparation and lighting. The atlas does not project donor photographs onto a different person's mesh. [NLM Visible Human color cryosections](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/photos.html) · [OpenStax lung anatomy](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-2-the-lungs) · [OpenStax heart anatomy](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-1-heart-anatomy)

Natural tissue uses existing surface geometry, material maps and normals. For vetted geometry without a color map, low-frequency procedural color and roughness follow the resting mesh as it moves. **Teaching colors** restores the lesson palette. Transparent layers, timing cues, labels and identification highlights carry their own instructional meanings. Microscopic and conceptual preparations retain their diagram colors. [Human Reference Atlas specimen library](https://humanatlas.io/3d-reference-library)

## Intact eyes

The eye pair uses the left and right sclera, cornea, iris, lens and retina from the registered Z-Anatomy/BodyParts3D model. The browser corrects the left eye’s inward-facing surfaces for display. The brown iris fibers and fine scleral vessels are adapted from MakeHuman’s CC0 brown eye texture. Brown is an illustrative choice. The pupil is an opening in the iris, and the catchlight follows the corneal surface as the view turns. For near focus, the globes turn inward together, the pupils narrow and the lenses thicken. The amount of rotation and change in pupil size are illustrative.
