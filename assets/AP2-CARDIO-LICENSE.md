# Cardiac and vascular specimen credits

## Human Reference Atlas, HuBMAP

The heart dissection uses **3D Reference Organ for Heart, Male v1.3** by Kristen Browne, derived from the National Library of Medicine Visible Human Project. The coronary and great-vessel surfaces come from **3D Reference Organ for Blood Vasculature, Male v1.3** by Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein. HuBMAP publishes both reference objects under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. [Heart record](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM564.WKNG.249) · [Vasculature record](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM473.XTPP.725) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Osteon used Blender 4.5 to select and combine the source surfaces in their original coordinates, simplify the trunk vessel mesh density and prepare material-neutral GLBs. The app supplies study colors. Conduction paths, the pericardial envelope and pressure-driven teaching leaflets are Osteon illustrations. The original four valve shapes and five papillary muscle groups appear in **Valves and supports**. The supplied source lacks independently labeled chordae. The left-sided pressure traces and leaflet motion illustrate an idealized beat.

## BodyParts3D

**BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science**, supplied the right lower-limb artery and bone geometry and the registered heart walls, sternum, ribs, costal cartilages and thoracic vertebrae. The 4.0 part-of archive and its FMA-to-element table were used together. DBCLS specifies CC BY 4.0 International on its current terms page. The downloaded 4.0 OBJ headers also carry an earlier CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan notice. Osteon additionally makes the two derivative GLBs available under that share-alike license. [Source data](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/download.html) · [Current DBCLS terms](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/lic.html) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.1/jp/)

Osteon converted millimeters to meters, grouped source elements under their anatomical names and assigned viewer colors. Distal foot elements keep their most specific source label when the source’s part-of hierarchy includes them under a larger vessel. The heart within the rib cage and the right-leg preparation each preserve a single donor’s spatial relationships. The detailed HRA heart dissection opens as a separate specimen.

## Mechanism reference

The original overlays, pressure and volume curves, case values, developmental drawings and flow diagrams support the learning objectives in OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e. The University of Minnesota’s Visible Heart Atlas provides views of valve anatomy and function for comparison.
