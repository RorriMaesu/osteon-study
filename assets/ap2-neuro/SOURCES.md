# Neural teaching specimens

## Ventricular system

Osteon extracted the ventricular spaces, pons, precentral gyri, caudate nuclei, putamina, globi pallidi, thalami, midbrain and medulla from its registered head nerve layer. The derivative retains the original body coordinates, uses reduced surface detail and adds teaching colors and cut planes. The colors in the obstruction exercise identify the drainage relationships upstream and downstream of an aqueduct block. The geometry retains its reference dimensions.

## Left eye

Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein, Human Reference Atlas, 3D Reference Organ for Eye (Male, Left), version 1.3. Osteon selected eleven anatomical surfaces, including the fovea, macula, optic disc and choroid, reduced the triangle counts, and opened a sagittal section in Blender. The interactive lens and ciliary-ring deformation illustrates the direction of change during accommodation. It is amplified for visibility and does not measure optical power. The specimen has its own magnified viewing space. [HRA record](https://apps.humanatlas.io/kg-explorer/ref-organ/eye-male-left/v1.3) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM769.RWTZ.963) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Inner ear

The separate middle-ear specimen contains the left malleus, incus and stapes extracted together from the Osteon Z-Anatomy skeleton using the same Blender transformation and coordinates. It retains the original anatomical relationships. See the BodyParts3D and Z-Anatomy credits above. The middle-ear derivative is distributed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Seyed-Ahmad Ahmadi, Theresa Marie Raiser, Ria Maxine Rühl, Virginia Lee Flanagin, Peter zu Eulenburg and the German Center for Vertigo and Balance Disorders, *IE-Map*, 2021. The atlas combines MRI-based geometry with registered surfaces from Romain David and colleagues' micro-CT atlas. Osteon extracted the slender segments and ampullae of the anterior, lateral and posterior semicircular ducts, utricle, saccule, scala vestibuli and scala tympani. The surfaces share the supplied IE-Map template coordinates, converted from millimetres to metres and simplified for web rendering. This is a magnified inner-ear specimen. [IE-Map dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/10625570) · [IE-Map paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7870663/) · [Original duct-model paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep32772) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
