# Peripheral blood specimens

The field photographs are from Andrea Acevedo, Anna Merino, Santiago Alférez, Ángel Molina, Laura Boldú and José Rodellar (2020), *A dataset for microscopic peripheral blood cell images for development of automatic recognition systems*, v1, Hospital Clínic de Barcelona. Clinical pathologists assigned the original class labels. These are single cells and fragments pictured in normal peripheral blood, including platelets. The pictures were converted from JPEG to WebP. [Dataset and license](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/snkd93bnjr/1) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [Source filenames](specimens.json).

Each of the six classes has three images for the microscopy activity and a fourth reserved for independent assessment. The exam and practice image pools have no images in common.

The labeled teaching plate comes from OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, Fig. 18.13. The micrographs are credited to the Regents of University of Michigan Medical School © 2012. The complete teaching plate was converted to WebP. [Figure and credits](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets) · [Textbook license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/).

## Mixed-field exercises

**normal-adult-field.webp** shows the Giemsa-stained peripheral smear photographed by Keith Chambers for the SCOOTER Project. Resized, converted to WebP and displayed with interactive overlays. [Original photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Normal_Adult_Blood_Smear.JPG) · [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).

**phil-5864.webp** comes from CDC and Steven Glenn (1979). The differential exercise displays a selected portion and counts only complete leukocytes. **phil-15133.webp** is the labeled comparison field by CDC and Dr. Candler Ballard (1974). Both are public-domain photographs, converted to WebP. [Original free CDC field 5864](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/Details.aspx?pid=5864) · [Original free CDC field 15133](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/Details.aspx?pid=15133).

## Three-dimensional formed elements

The biconcave erythrocyte, platelet, and five leukocyte classes are original Osteon teaching reconstructions. Their relative sizes are approximate. The leukocyte surfaces are translucent so students can inspect the nuclei and cytoplasmic granules. The separate blood micrographs show stained tissue. [OpenStax §18.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes) · [OpenStax §18.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
