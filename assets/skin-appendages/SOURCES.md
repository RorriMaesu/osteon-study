# Hair-bearing skin and its appendages

Osteon created this magnified, longitudinal teaching cutaway in Blender 4.5. The model represents hair-bearing thin skin. The retained tissue block shows the epidermis, dermis and subcutaneous bed. Three examples share this specimen.

- A secretory coil continues into an eccrine duct. Its superficial segment spirals through the epidermis and opens through its own pore. The tracer follows the centerline used to construct the tubule.
- Lobules of a sebaceous gland drain into the upper follicle. A second tracer follows the short side duct and then ascends alongside the hair shaft.
- An arrector pili muscle attaches to the follicle below the sebaceous gland. Its contraction shortens and thickens the muscle, moving the follicle and hair toward an upright position. The reconstruction holds the superficial attachment fixed. Hair angle, smooth-muscle shortening, fluid bead sizes and elapsed time are illustrative.

The separate gross skin map shows body location. This local close-up combines anatomy typically seen across several histology sections. Local tissue displacement, individual cells, and sweating rates are outside its scope.

The anatomical relationships and secretory mechanisms follow [OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, §5.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-2-accessory-structures-of-the-skin). Human scalp and sweat-gland micrographs are available in [Duke University’s integumentary histology laboratory](https://histology.oit.duke.edu/DPT/Skin/skinHistology.html). The Human Reference Atlas documents its separate body-scale skin specimen in the [3D Reference Object Library](https://humanatlas.io/3d-reference-library).
