## What the exam review changed

1. **Check what can actually appear on an exam.** The unit mocks already sampled every numbered section in their units. The original three cumulative forms together sampled 46 of 49 winter sections and 49 of 53 spring sections as the primary topic of a question. A topic mentioned in an explanation earned no coverage credit in this count. The revised cumulative forms distribute those sections across A, B and C. An individual final still samples the course.
2. **Test a different decision on the next attempt.** Winter students could answer two blood-typing questions by recognizing the same B-positive result. Another pair used the same change in albumin to test fluid exchange. Spring students saw the same four integrated short-answer scenarios on all three finals. The revised banks vary what students must predict, identify, calculate or explain, and link each answer to its primary reading.
3. **Assess laboratory and reasoning skills alongside recall.** Use a tracing, specimen, measured result or tissue image to identify a structure, then predict a consequence. Winter examples include cerebral circulation, nonvisual senses and blood compatibility. Spring examples include renal drainage, nutrient transport and acid–base compensation. Spring weeks 1–10 each offer three independently written forms. Their cases use different observations from the unit mocks so students can test the same concepts again with fresh evidence. The existing practicals provide a separate opportunity to identify structures. [Oregon BI 232Z outcomes](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/about/transfer/Documents/Transfer-Resources/transfer-common-course-numbering/2025-CCNAP-for-BI-BIOL-BIOL-232Z.pdf) · [Oregon BI 233Z outcomes](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/about/transfer/Documents/Transfer-Resources/transfer-common-course-numbering/2025-CCNAP-for-BI-BIOL-BIOL-233Z.pdf)
4. **Put each checkpoint after its readings.** Each numbered section has a first study week. A week spanning two units can introduce the next unit while the unit mock checks only the completed one. Week 11 is reserved for integrating prior work. For example, in winter trace a baroreflex from stimulus to effector. In spring explain how ventilation and renal handling of bicarbonate influence the same acid–base disturbance. [OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/preface)
5. **Make results actionable.** The chapter breakdown reports the number of questions, points earned, possible points and points pending review. A cross-system question counts once under its primary section. A low score from a single question is a reason to revisit that reading and try another case. Saved attempts retain the questions and rubrics from the day they were started. Local AI review and learner confirmation remain available for eligible answers.

| Coverage measure | A&P II | A&P III |

| Numbered sections in the course | 49 | 53 |
| Distinct primary sections across the three cumulative forms, before | 46 | 49 |
| Distinct primary sections across the three cumulative forms, after | 49 | 53 |
| Distinct primary sections on each revised cumulative form, A / B / C | 36 / 35 / 33 | 32 / 36 / 36 |

**Winter.** The independent chapter question bank expanded from 147 to 196 items, four per section. Another 86 cases support the mock forms. Across the 15 unit and cumulative forms, exact repeat appearances of lesson multiple-choice prompts fell from 163 to zero. In a reverse blood-typing case, students must infer the patient’s ABO group from the plasma reaction with known red cells. The cumulative forms spread their coverage across neuroanatomy, special senses, endocrine regulation, blood and circulation.

**Spring.** The four short-answer cases repeated on every final became 12 distinct cases across three forms. Students now work from paired CO₂ production and ventilation measurements, calculate filtered load and net reabsorption, and interpret an ovarian tissue observation. The papillary drainage station returns students to gross kidney anatomy (§25.3), and the suckling question returns them to lactation (§28.6). The separate 32-question cumulative exam includes inheritance on every attempt. The winter term runs January 4–March 20 and spring runs March 29–June 11, 2027. The sequence keeps prerequisite ideas close to their applications. Students encounter voltage changes before sensory pathways, endocrine signaling before gland feedback, and blood and valves before pressure-driven flow. In spring they study filtration before acid–base compensation and reproductive cycles before implantation. Weeks that introduce a second chapter mark which readings the unit mock covers. Students whose classes run in a different order can still select a chapter exam directly.

The questions, section distribution and software behavior can be checked before a course begins. How difficult each form is for students, and how well any score predicts the instructor’s exam, require actual student performance and instructor feedback.
