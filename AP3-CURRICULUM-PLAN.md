# Osteon • A&P III curriculum and model plan

**Planning baseline** · Osteon revision `a12002c77e41` · November 27, 2026

A&P I owns Chapters 1–11, A&P II owns 12–20, and A&P III owns 21–28. The sequence below is a proposed build and study order. McNulty’s weekly assignments, practical list and exam weights are still unconfirmed. UCC’s published biology description names nutrition, metabolism and genetics. Oregon’s approved BI 233Z outcomes also call for acid–base regulation, development, lab identification, feedback and application across systems. Chapter 21 belongs to spring even though OpenStax places it at the end of the same textbook unit as blood and circulation. ## What the spring student should be able to do

Plan for **eight chapters and all 53 numbered sections**. Give each section an observable objective, a worked example, an independent task, feedback and a source. Within each chapter, give students a short sequence they can repeat with labels hidden.

1. **Find it.** Locate a structure in the body, then identify it in a section, micrograph or second view. Keep left/right, scale and section plane visible.
2. **Trace it.** Follow a substance, cell, signal or force. Let the student pick the next stop before revealing an arrow.
3. **Predict it.** Change one condition, commit to a prediction, then scrub through the result. Make the quantity that stays fixed explicit.
4. **Prove it.** Use an unfamiliar image, trace or case. Ask which observation supports the answer. Save independent responses and assisted retries separately.

### 21 · Lymphatic system and immunity • §§21.1–7

**Teach in order.** Start with interstitial fluid, lymphatic capillaries, collecting vessels, nodes, trunks, ducts and the return to veins. Distinguish nodes, spleen, thymus, tonsils and marrow by what enters them and what happens there. Move from barriers and innate responses to T cells, B cells, antibodies and immune memory. Apply recognition and regulation to deficient or excessive responses, transplantation and cancer surveillance.

**Build the lab.** Reuse the regional node atlas and existing spleen, marrow and thymus views. Add selectable drainage territories, both terminal ducts and venous angles. Offer a node cutaway with entry on the convex surface, sinuses and exit at the hilum. Show a typical pathway with arrows the learner can turn off. Mark drawn connections as illustrative wherever node-to-vessel continuity is unverified. Use matched lymph-node, spleen and thymus histology for independent identification.

**Test it.** Compare lymph return from the right hand and left foot. Have the student place each in its usual duct, locate where it enters venous blood and distinguish a node filtering lymph from a spleen filtering blood. Pair this station with an unseen primary/secondary antibody-response graph. [OpenStax Chapter 21](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-chapter-review)

### 22 · Respiratory system • §§22.1–7

**Teach in order.** Trace the conducting passages to the respiratory zone. Relate pleura and diaphragm to pressure and airflow, then distinguish ventilation, gas exchange and transport in blood. Include breathing control, exercise, altitude, lung development and surfactant.

**Build the lab.** Reuse the thorax, nasal/laryngeal context and pulmonary vessels. Fit both lungs, fissures, five lobes, main bronchi and diaphragm using matching body coordinates. Add separate pleural and alveolar-capillary cutaways. Synchronize a slow breathing cycle with volume, alveolar pressure, intrapleural pressure and airflow. Allow pause at end-inspiration when airflow reaches zero. Pair regional anatomy with an alveoli-versus-bronchiole micrograph.

**Test it.** Give two breathing patterns with the same minute ventilation, supply dead-space volume, then ask which delivers more fresh air to alveoli. Use an unseen spirogram and ask which quantities it can measure directly. Residual volume requires another method. [OpenStax Chapter 22](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-chapter-review)

### 23 · Digestive system • §§23.1–7

**Teach in order.** Begin with alimentary canal layers, motility and neural/hormonal control. Trace food through mouth, pharynx, esophagus, stomach and intestines. Follow liver, gallbladder and pancreatic secretions to the duodenum. End with where macronutrients are digested and where their products go.

**Build the lab.** Extend the existing abdominal organs and enteric-wall cutaway with an esophagus-to-rectum route, the biliary/pancreatic ducts and a villus containing capillaries and a central lacteal. Give lumen, portal blood and lymph separate selectable paths. Use real tissue sections to distinguish stomach, small intestine and colon after labels disappear.

**Test it.** Follow absorbed glucose through portal blood to the liver. Start again with a long-chain dietary fatty acid, follow its incorporation into a chylomicron and trace its first entry into venous blood. Ask learners to point to where the routes diverge. [OpenStax Chapter 23](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-chapter-review)

### 24 · Nutrition and metabolism • §§24.1–7

**Teach in order.** Establish anabolism, catabolism, energy transfer and enzyme roles. Compare carbohydrate, lipid and protein handling, fed and postabsorptive states, thermoregulation, nutrients and diet. Follow carbon, amino nitrogen and energy as separate quantities.

**Build the lab.** Reuse liver, skeletal muscle, pancreas and gut as body locations. Connect them to a cell view that distinguishes cytosol from mitochondrial compartments. Build a fed/fasting fuel-flow diagram, a heat-balance activity and a nutrient-function check. Use labeled arrows and a graph here, since manipulating a liver surface alone cannot teach a change in flux.

**Test it.** Switch from after a meal to an overnight fast and predict changes in liver glycogen use, glucose export and adipose fuel release. Give one unexplored nutrient or heat-loss example on the exam. Distinguish a nutrient’s energy contribution from its role as a cofactor. [OpenStax Chapter 24](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/24-chapter-review)

### 25 · Urinary system • §§25.1–10

**Teach in order.** Begin with urine properties and the outflow tract. Move into gross kidney anatomy, renal histology and filtration. Then teach reabsorption, secretion, regulation of renal perfusion, endocrine control, fluid and solute handling, and the kidney’s roles in blood pressure, erythropoiesis and calcitriol production.

**Build the lab.** Fit paired kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra. Cut one kidney to expose cortex, medulla, pyramids, papillae, calyces and pelvis. Keep an enlarged nephron and glomerulus separately selectable. Trace blood through afferent arteriole, glomerulus, efferent arteriole and downstream capillaries, alongside filtrate from capsular space to collecting duct. Include cortical and juxtamedullary examples and a medullary gradient view. Give a collecting duct its actual position in a shared drainage system.

**Test it.** Identify the renal corpuscle on an unfamiliar cortical section. Compare two routes whose starting points are close in 3D, then calculate excretion from filtration, reabsorption and secretion. As a worked case, filtering 125 mg/min, reabsorbing 120 mg/min and secreting zero gives 5 mg/min excreted. [OpenStax Chapter 25](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-chapter-review)

### 26 · Fluid, electrolyte and acid–base balance • §§26.1–5

**Teach in order.** Distinguish intracellular, interstitial and plasma compartments. Predict water movement from tonicity, then connect water and electrolyte regulation to feedback. Teach buffers, pulmonary CO₂ control, renal bicarbonate handling, the four primary acid–base disturbances and the direction of expected compensation.

**Build the lab.** Link existing vessels and blood cells to the new lung and nephron. Give learners adjustable compartment volumes and effective solute concentrations. Use a time plot to separate immediate buffering, respiratory responses and slower renal responses. Include units, reference ranges and a static frame for every prediction.

**Test it.** Predict the direction of water movement after an extracellular tonicity change. On a separate fictional blood-gas panel, identify the primary disturbance and predict what the compensating organ will do. Use internally consistent examples and ask how the values support the answer. [OpenStax Chapter 26](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/26-chapter-review)

### 27 · Reproductive system • §§27.1–3

**Teach in order.** Connect testicular and ovarian anatomy to gamete formation, transport and hormone feedback. Synchronize the ovarian and uterine cycles. Cover embryonic reproductive differentiation, descent and changes at puberty.

**Build the lab.** Start with both existing pelvic preparations, testes, epididymides, reproductive ducts, accessory glands, ovaries, uterine tubes and uterus. Check the entire sperm route including ejaculatory ducts and urethra. For pelvic orientation add missing vagina, cervix, external structures and relationships to bladder and rectum as needed. Pair gross targets with seminiferous tubule, follicle and endometrium sections. Give the hormone plot and tissue images a common time cursor. Show menstrual cycle length as variable.

**Test it.** Identify a follicle from an unfamiliar image and use a previously unseen hormone trace to predict the next ovarian and uterine events. Test sperm transport with one step hidden and ask how it differs from the route of urine. [OpenStax Chapter 27](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-chapter-review)

### 28 · Development and inheritance • §§28.1–7

**Teach in order.** Trace fertilization, cleavage, implantation, germ layers, embryonic folding and fetal development. Connect placental exchange to pregnancy, labor, newborn transition and lactation. Teach inheritance through pedigrees and conditional probabilities.

**Build the lab.** Build separate views for cleavage/implantation, selected staged embryos, fetal support structures, childbirth orientation and a lactating mammary unit. Reuse the full-term placenta and fetal circulation animation for the late-stage views. Label embryonic age after fertilization separately from obstetric gestational age. Show specimens at equal scale first, then allow enlargement for landmarks. The placenta view should keep maternal and fetal circulations distinguishable at the exchange surface.

**Test it.** Sequence unfamiliar developmental images using visible features. Predict how first breaths and cord clamping change flow, with separate time scales for functional and anatomical closure. Include a milk production/ejection comparison and a pedigree with stated inheritance assumptions. [OpenStax Chapter 28](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/28-chapter-review)

## Release order and evidence of readiness

| Phase | Work | What a student should be able to demonstrate before release |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Course home and Chapter 21 pilot | Add A&P III to the course picker. Give it its own resume, progress, notes, tutor context, practicals and exam results. Keep the Atlas shared and deep-link back to the originating lesson. Deliver the entire Chapter 21 learning sequence first. | From a phone, trace one drainage territory, inspect a node, take a blind practical, close the app, and find that result again under III. Previously saved fall and winter results still open with their original scoring. |
| 2 · Breathing | Fit the thorax and deliver Chapter 22 from airway to alveolus to spirogram. | Independently locate a lobe, predict airflow after a pressure change and read a fresh trace with the animation paused. |
| 3 · Food to fuel | Deliver Chapter 23’s gut route and Chapter 24’s fed/fasting activity together. | Trace two nutrients to their different first destinations, then explain how the liver handles one of them as feeding state changes. |
| 4 · Kidney to pH | Deliver Chapter 25 before Chapter 26. Integrate with the spring lung and the winter circulatory model. | Trace blood and filtrate without crossing into the wrong compartment. Predict the renal and respiratory responses to a new disturbance at appropriate time scales. |
| 5 · Reproduction and development | Release the pelvic/hormone lab before the staged embryo, placenta, neonatal transition and inheritance labs. | Use tissue and hormone evidence to infer cycle phase, distinguish placental flows, and justify a predicted inheritance probability on a new pedigree. |
| 6 · Spring exams and cross-system practical | Give each chapter its own exam, then deliver a cumulative written form and a practical with coverage across all eight chapters. Version the item pools and saved reports. | Two attempts draw different images, values, targets or case details for the same objectives. Students can find the source, review a miss privately and retrieve their original submitted result. |

A possible study schedule uses one block each for Chapters 21–24, two for Chapter 25, one each for Chapters 26 and 27, two for Chapter 28 and one mixed review. Chapter 25 earns a second block because tracing a kidney and reasoning about tubular transport are distinct skills. Spread retrieval across blocks, for example by bringing Chapter 21 lymph back during fat absorption and Chapter 22 gas exchange back during acid–base balance.

**Exam design.** For every chapter, sample structure identification, mechanism or route, and interpretation of new evidence. For the cumulative practical, give each chapter a station appropriate to its material. Metabolism and acid–base balance deserve data stations. Score the identification and the reasoning separately. Keep worked examples out of the independent pool. Use alternate views plus different targets or datasets. Accept equivalent units, minor spelling errors and established anatomical synonyms while preserving distinctions such as afferent/efferent. Allow students to complete the exams with the answer key alone. Let the optional local Socratic tutor ask for the observation supporting a missed inference, store the original answer, and link the resulting study note back to the exact lesson. Add an instructor-priority overlay when the syllabus arrives.

**The first publishable milestone is Chapter 21**, from the course picker through an independently scored result. It tests the entire learning path using assets already in Osteon and gives classmates something meaningful to try before the larger organ imports.
