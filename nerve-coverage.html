How to interpret the surfaces

Source surface means an attributed adult reference geometry. It can represent a branch group, a modeled route or an individually named structure. Available path stops describe study regions along the modeled portion. The extent and branch patterns can vary between people.

The original nerve data and the position references come from Z-Anatomy and BodyParts3D. Z-Anatomy credits the University of Dundee cranial nerve model among its sources.

Selected additional right-sided surfaces come from the Leiden UMC brachial plexus and lower-limb Open3DModel. Their detail panels identify the source and any modeled segment limits.

Coverage still in progress

  • Spinal levels. T1–T11 intercostal nerves are individually selectable on both sides. These are the thoracic ventral rami. Some spinal roots and dorsal root ganglia are still grouped. The SPARC spinal scaffold labels all 31 levels, including the coccygeal nerve. Its positions require a separate fit to Osteon’s vertebral exits.
  • Cranial branches. Both sides of cranial nerves I–XII are modeled. Individual skull-base passages and smaller extracranial branches need further verification. The modeled vagus extent differs by side.
  • Autonomic and organ nerves. The layer includes portions of the sympathetic trunks and vagus. Coverage of the recurrent laryngeal and splanchnic nerves, pelvic autonomic pathways, and intramural networks is still incomplete. The right phrenic nerve currently reaches the thoracic inlet in 3D.
  • Peripheral detail. Some distal branches and the opposite side of selected right-sided additions still need independent sources.

Route checks

In Blender, the right radial nerve surface was within about 1 mm of the atlas’s radial groove reference and the right sciatic nerve surface was within about 1 mm of the greater sciatic foramen reference. These are checks against individual reference points. The courses still need to be reviewed along their full length before they are used as precision targets.

The right vagus at the skull base and the facial and vestibulocochlear nerves near the internal acoustic meatus need further alignment review. The vagus route guide begins in the neck.
