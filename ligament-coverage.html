Ligament coverage

Turn on Ligaments in the atlas to see the gold surfaces in your current view. To find a specific ligament, choose the Ligaments tab in Browse. Selecting a result opens a close view with surrounding anatomy faded.

What the atlas contains

323 selectable surfaces across the head, spine, rib cage, pelvis, and upper and lower limbs. The count includes 303 ligament surfaces, 10 retinacula, 8 fibrous membranes, and 2 oblique cords. Left and right surfaces are listed separately. Some repeated small bands are grouped into a single selectable mesh.

Examples include the elbow collateral and radial annular ligaments, wrist and hand ligaments, hip capsular ligaments, knee cruciates and collaterals, and ankle and foot ligaments.

How the models were placed

Most surfaces come from the Z-Anatomy specimen that provides Osteon’s skeleton. Their original coordinates were preserved during export. The transverse ligament of the atlas and both patellar ligaments are labeled teaching reconstructions. Their attachment regions were checked against the adjacent Osteon bones, and the atlantal ligament was also checked against published measurements.

See Z-Anatomy, the upper-cervical dissection and MRI study, and OpenStax 9.6.

Gaps in the 3D layer

  • Skull and ear. The medial temporomandibular ligament, periodontal ligaments, and the small ligaments of the ear ossicles have no individual surfaces here.
  • Upper cervical spine. The alar and apical ligaments, vertical cruciform bands, and tectorial and atlanto-occipital membranes are still missing.
  • Knee and ankle. The meniscofemoral and medial patellofemoral ligaments and anterior tibiotalar component lack individual surfaces in this release.
  • Visceral structures. Organ-specific peritoneal and pelvic suspensory ligaments require their corresponding organ models.

The layer covers the available surfaces from this specimen. Attachment outlines and anatomical variants require further verification. See FIPAT’s anatomical terminology for the broader inventory.
